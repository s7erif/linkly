# Tapp Landing Page Rules

Version: 1.0

---

# Purpose

The landing page is not a marketing page.

It is a guided product experience.

The visitor should naturally move from curiosity
to understanding
to trust
to action.

Every section has exactly one responsibility.

---

# Landing Philosophy

Do not sell.

Demonstrate.

Do not overwhelm.

Educate.

Do not impress with effects.

Impress with clarity.

The product should explain itself.

---

# User Journey

Every visitor follows this journey:

Notice

↓

Understand

↓

Imagine

↓

Trust

↓

Start

Never skip a step.

---

# Page Structure

The landing page should follow this order.

1. Hero

2. Social Proof

3. Problem

4. Solution

5. Features

6. How It Works

7. Product Showcase

8. Testimonials

9. Pricing

10. FAQ

11. Final CTA

Nothing should appear randomly.

---

# Hero

Purpose

Explain the product immediately.

The visitor should understand Tapp in less than three seconds.

Hero answers:

"What is this?"

Never answer:

"Why us?"

That comes later.

---

Hero Requirements

One dominant visual.

One headline.

One supporting paragraph.

Primary CTA.

Secondary CTA.

Product demonstration.

Minimal distractions.

No dashboard screenshots.

No generic phone mockups.

---

Hero Story

The product should demonstrate itself.

Good example:

An NFC card approaches a phone.

The phone reacts.

The profile appears.

The interaction becomes the message.

---

# Social Proof

Purpose

Reduce uncertainty.

Examples

Trusted by

Companies

Creators

Businesses

Review count

Cards created

Connections made

Keep this section calm.

Do not exaggerate numbers.

---

# Problem Section

Purpose

Make the visitor recognize their current problem.

Example

Paper business cards are forgotten.

Contact information gets lost.

Links are difficult to remember.

Networking feels outdated.

Focus on real frustrations.

Not marketing language.

---

# Solution Section

Purpose

Present Tapp as the natural solution.

Do not compare against competitors.

Simply demonstrate a better experience.

---

# Features

Purpose

Explain capabilities.

Every feature must answer:

"What can I actually do?"

Avoid technical language.

Describe outcomes.

Instead of:

"NFC"

Say:

"Share your profile with one tap."

---

Feature Rules

Maximum six features.

Each feature:

One icon.

One title.

One short description.

Optional visual.

No long paragraphs.

---

# How It Works

Purpose

Remove uncertainty.

Maximum:

Three or four steps.

Each step should be understandable within five seconds.

Good example

Create your profile.

Customize your card.

Share with one tap.

Grow your network.

---

# Product Showcase

Purpose

Show real product screens.

Not placeholders.

Not wireframes.

Not fake dashboards.

Demonstrate real interactions.

Visitors should imagine themselves using the product.

---

# Testimonials

Purpose

Create emotional trust.

Use real people.

Real names.

Real companies.

Real profile photos.

Avoid anonymous reviews.

---

# Pricing

Purpose

Reduce decision friction.

Simple.

Transparent.

No hidden information.

Highlight the recommended plan.

Never overload with comparison tables.

---

# FAQ

Purpose

Answer final objections.

Only answer real questions.

Avoid writing FAQs simply to fill space.

---

# Final CTA

Purpose

Encourage action.

Repeat the value.

Do not introduce new information.

Keep it simple.

One headline.

One sentence.

One CTA.

---

# Navigation

Always visible.

Minimal.

Never crowded.

Logo.

Navigation.

Primary CTA.

Nothing else.

---

# Content Rules

Use short paragraphs.

Use short headlines.

Maximum readability.

Avoid buzzwords.

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

Use active voice.

Write like a human.

Not like marketing.

---

# Section Rhythm

Every section should breathe.

Use generous spacing.

One visual focus.

One content focus.

Avoid visual overload.

---

# Visual Balance

Alternate layouts naturally.

Do not repeat identical section structures.

Create rhythm.

Large visual.

Small visual.

Centered.

Split layout.

Editorial composition.

---

# Calls To Action

Every CTA must describe the outcome.

Bad

Get Started

Learn More

Click Here

Better

Create Your Card

Get Your NFC Card

Share Your Profile

Start Free

---

# Trust Signals

Use naturally.

Examples

SSL

Privacy

Secure Authentication

Fast Loading

Responsive

Professional Support

Never overload trust badges.

---

# Performance

Avoid heavy graphics.

Optimize images.

Lazy load sections.

Animations should never reduce performance.

Target 60 FPS.

---

# Accessibility

High contrast.

Keyboard navigation.

Visible focus states.

Readable typography.

Proper semantic HTML.

Accessible animations.

---

# Responsive Design

The mobile experience is not a smaller desktop.

Redesign layouts when necessary.

Prioritize readability.

Reduce complexity.

Preserve storytelling.

---

# SEO

Every section should contain meaningful content.

Never stuff keywords.

Headings should form a logical hierarchy.

Use descriptive alt text.

Optimize metadata.

---

# Final Quality Checklist

Before shipping ask:

Can someone understand the product in three seconds?

Does every section have exactly one purpose?

Can anything be removed?

Does the page feel calm?

Does it feel premium?

Does it build trust?

Would someone remember it tomorrow?

If the answer is no,

continue refining.

---

# Non-Negotiable Rules

Never build generic SaaS layouts.

Never use placeholder content.

Never decorate without purpose.

Never create visual noise.

Never sacrifice clarity.

Always tell a story.

Always demonstrate the product.

Always design for trust.

Always make the next action obvious.

---

End of Landing Rules
